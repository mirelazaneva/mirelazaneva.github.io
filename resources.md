A lightly curated list of resources I've found helpful.

<details>
  <summary><strong>Scientific writing</strong></summary>
  <p>The writing I admire most is clear, transparent, and accessible. 
  Here is a paper I find helpful in terms of why we need to just get to the point:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><a href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0735275117709046">Fuck Nuance</a></li>
  </ul>
  <p>I find developing good writing easier when learning by example. And so, I recommend reading voraciously. Here are papers I find have vivid language, great analogies, and make clear points powerfully:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><a href="https://myscp.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1016/j.jcps.2011.02.002">If money doesn't make you happy, then you probably aren't spending it right</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://example.com">PAPER TITLE 3</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://example.com">PAPER TITLE 4</a></li>
  </ul>
</details>


<details>
  <summary><strong>Scientific creativity</strong></summary>
  <ul>
    <li><a href="https://night-science.org/">Night Science</a>. Itai Yanai and Martin Lercher's project on the creative, exploratory side of research. I recommend both the site and the podcast, which is great when on the go.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Some night science papers:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-023-02074-2">It takes two to think</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://www.science.org/doi/full/10.1126/science.adr4539">Teach creativity in science higher education</a></li>
    <li><a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-023-01736-5">Make science disruptive again</a></li>
  </ul>
</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>Data analysis & data viz (R)</strong></summary>
  <ul>
    <li><a href="https://r4ds.hadley.nz/">R for Data Science (2e)</a>. A free online book on importing, tidying, transforming, and visualizing data with the tidyverse.</li>
    <li><a href="https://informationisbeautiful.net/">Information is Beautiful</a>. David McCandless's visualizations, good for inspiration on how to make data presentation clear and appealing.</li>
    <li><a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/dataisbeautiful/">r/dataisbeautiful</a>. A community sharing data vizualisations, good for browsing examples.</li>
    <li><a href="https://lakens.github.io/statistical_inferences/">Improving Your Statistical Inferences</a>. Daniel Lakens' free online textbook on statistics for research.</li>
    <li><a href="https://github.com/rmcelreath/stat_rethinking_2024">Statistical Rethinking</a>. Richard McElreath's statistics course (youtube links).</li>
  </ul>
</details>


<details>
  <summary><strong>Open science</strong></summary>
  <ul>
    <li><a href="https://www.i4replication.org/">Institute for Replication (I4R)</a>. Joining a replication game is great for anyone who wants to have more structure and learn-by-doing, since you work in groups with others and use templated reports.</li>
    <li><a href="https://forrt.org/">FORRT</a>. A community and set of resources for teaching and learning open and reproducible research. Join on Slack!</li>
    <li><a href="https://improvingpsych.org/">Improving Psych</a>. A research society & hub for efforts to improve psychological science.</li>

  </ul>
</details>
